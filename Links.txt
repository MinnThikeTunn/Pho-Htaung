



1. Best 100% Free Tools for Drawing & Editing





LibreSprite (libresprite.github.io)



Best for: Pixel art characters, item icons, and frame-by-frame animation.



Why use it: Free open-source clone of Aseprite with layers, palettes, and onion skinning.





Piskel (piskelapp.com)



Best for: Quick browser-based pixel edits (zero download).



Why use it: Draw pixel by pixel, resize sprites, test animations in real time.





Krita (krita.org)



Best for: Hand-drawn, painterly art (Machinarium rusty backgrounds and characters).



Why use it: Professional-grade brush engine, texture brushes, completely free and open source.





Inkscape (inkscape.org)



Best for: Modular 2D puppet characters (vector pieces like arms, heads, and legs).



Why use it: Infinite scaling without blurriness; easy to export individual body parts.





2. How to Edit an Existing Asset (Adding Details / Altering Pixels)





Step 1: Open with Transparency intact



Import your .png into LibreSprite, Piskel, or Krita. Never save as .jpg (it destroys transparency).





Step 2: Add a New Layer



Never draw directly over the original image. Create Layer 2 above the original asset.





Step 3: Sample the Palette (Eyedropper Tool)



Press I (Eyedropper) to pick existing outline and shadow colors from the asset so your new pixels blend in naturally.





Step 4: Draw with a 1-Pixel Pencil



Keep your brush size locked to 1 pixel for pixel art. Avoid soft/feathered brushes.





Step 5: Export as PNG



Hide any background guide layer and export as transparent 32-bit PNG.





3. How to Make a Brand-New Character from Scratch (5-Step Workflow)





Step 1: Start with a Small Canvas



Do not start huge. Draw your character on a 32x32 or 64x64 pixel canvas first to keep details manageable.





Step 2: Block Out the Silhouette First



Use a single solid color (like dark gray). Focus only on the outline and posture—if the character is not recognizable by silhouette alone, the design will feel flat.





Step 3: Add Flat Base Colors



Fill in skin, clothes, and accessories with flat mid-tone colors. Limit your palette to 3–5 main colors.





Step 4: Apply 2-Tone Shading



Pick a light source (usually top-left). Add one darker shade for cast shadows and one lighter shade for highlights.





Step 5: Separate Body Parts on Different Layers (For Animation)



If building a modular character like Machinarium or Phoe Htaung: keep Head, Torso, Upper Arm, Forearm, and Legs on separate layers so you can pivot and animate them independently.





4. Standard Canvas Sizes Cheat Sheet





Item Icons (Inventory): 16x16, 24x24, or 32x32 pixels (retro) | 64x64 to 128x128 (detailed)



Characters: 32x32 (mini retro) | 64x64 to 128x128 (standard 2D) | 256x256 to 512x512 (high-res hand-drawn)



Level Backgrounds: 320x180 (upscaled 16:9 pixel art) | 1920x1080 (full HD hand-drawn)
